# 0047 — What a helper grants, and what a story's authors may do

**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Status:** accepted (D-1 and D-2, owner 2026-10-18); written into `/terms` by item 2.2
**Amends:** nothing in the SRS; makes FR-9.1, FR-10.3 and FR-14.4 legally explicit

## Why

The product's whole loop is one person's words going into another person's
manuscript. The SRS says credit is permanent (FR-9.1), that a public story may
be spun off on Draftboard (FR-10.3), and that authors may publish anywhere
(FR-14.4), but never says who owns what, or what a helper has agreed to by
pressing Send. A terms page cannot be written without an answer, and the
answer should be the one the code already keeps.

## The decision

**A helper keeps the copyright** in every suggestion and idea they write. By
sending it they grant the story's authors a permanent, worldwide,
non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to use, edit and publish it as part of
that story, in any form and anywhere, and they grant Draftboard a licence to
store and show it in order to run the service, including in backups and in
the authors' exports. The grant is made at sending. Accepting is when the
words enter the story; passing leaves the helper's copy where it was.

**Credit is the condition the product keeps, not the licence.** The owner's
words: credit comes only when a suggestion is accepted into the story. A
credit line is permanent (FR-9.1) and travels with the words into spin-offs
and exports; it is asked of authors who publish elsewhere, not enforced
(FR-14.4). The authors of a story owe no credit for their own words on it
(decision 0041).

**A story's authors keep the copyright in their story.** Draftboard takes
only the licence it needs to store, show, back up and export it.

**A spin-off** of a public story may be made by anyone signed in, on
Draftboard, without asking (FR-10.3); the original's author sees who did.
Publishing a spin-off outside Draftboard needs the permission of the authors
of the words it carries over, because a licence to the story's authors is not
a licence to everyone who copies the story (D-2). Decision 0044 adds a
published, read-only edition inside Draftboard, which needs no permission.

**Everything a helper sends is visible** to whoever can read the story
(decision 0020), and what was sent while a story was private stays with the
people who could read it then (decisions 0007 and 0039).

## What this does not change

Who may read a story (the matrix, SRS §3.2); what a report, a suspension or a
hide does to a contribution (decisions 0018 and 0045): a hidden suggestion
is still the helper's, and its licence stands.

## Consequences

The terms page says exactly this, in plainer words, and nothing else about
rights. A helper who wants their words out of a story has the erasure of
their contribution record (decision 0013), which removes the credit, not the
licence: the words were accepted into somebody's manuscript, and the licence
is what makes that manuscript publishable.
