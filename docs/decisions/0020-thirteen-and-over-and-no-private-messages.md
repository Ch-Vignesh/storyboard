# 0020 — Thirteen and over, and no private messages

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Status:** accepted
**Resolves:** OD-7 (age policy), which the SRS calls not optional before launch

## The question

OD-7: "Young writers are a large part of this audience. Under-16 handling, and
whether an adult stranger should be able to message them, needs a policy before
launch, not after."

## The decision

Two halves, and the second is what makes the first safe.

**Thirteen and over.** One checkbox at sign-up, stated in the community rules,
and no date of birth stored anywhere. The product does not ask for an age it
cannot verify and would then have to protect.

**No private messages, ever.** Not "not yet" — this is now a property of the
design, and adding a direct-message feature later would reopen OD-7 rather than
extend it. Every way one person can reach another here is public and attached to
a piece of work:

| Surface                | Who sees it                                          |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| A contribution request | anyone who can read the storyboard                   |
| A suggestion           | the author, and the request thread                   |
| An idea, and replies   | the request thread                                   |
| A pass, with its chip  | the contributor, from a fixed list (FR-6.10)         |
| A notification         | generated by the product, never composed by a person |

There is no field anywhere in which an adult can write a sentence of their own
choosing that only a child will read.

## Why this rather than sixteen

Sixteen would clear GDPR Article 8 everywhere in the EU without any further
thought, and it was rejected because it excludes exactly the writers the SRS
says this product is for. A fifteen-year-old with a novel is the person the
whole thing was designed around.

Thirteen is the floor because it is COPPA's, and below it the obligations change
in kind rather than in degree.

## Why not an under-16 mode

The third option was to store an age band and default under-16 accounts to
private, out of browse and out of the digests. It was rejected for three
reasons, in order of weight.

It asks for a date of birth — data the product would then hold, protect, and
have to honour a deletion request for, in exchange for a protection the no-DM
rule already provides.

It would make a young writer's work invisible by default, which is the opposite
of what somebody joins this for.

And the band goes stale. An account created at fifteen is sixteen a year later
and the row still says otherwise, so either there is a birthday job or the data
is quietly wrong.

## What this does not claim

It does not verify anybody's age. Nothing on the open web does, and saying so
plainly is better than a date picker that implies otherwise — the same reasoning
FR-13.6 applies to copying.

It does not make the product safe for a young writer in every respect. Public
writing attracts public replies, and a suggestion can be unkind inside the word
bounds. That is what FR-13.5's reporting and the community rules are for.

## What this constrains, in writing

**Any future feature that lets one person send another a message only they can
read reopens this decision.** That includes a private reply to a suggestion, a
co-author chat, and an "email this writer" button. If one is ever wanted, the
age policy has to be built first, not alongside.
