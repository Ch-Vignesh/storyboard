Chapter two

The registry

The registry office kept its windows shut against the salt, which meant the air inside had been the same air for eleven years. Ellery had learned to breathe shallowly in it. She set the folder on the counter and waited while the clerk finished a column of figures that could not possibly have needed finishing.

"Vessel name," he said, without looking up.

"It hasn't got one. That's the difficulty."

· · ·

He looked up then. The clerk had a face like a document that had been folded and unfolded too many times, and he used it now to convey that unnamed vessels were not a difficulty but a category error, and that she should go away and come back with a name.

Ellery did not go away. She had come three hundred miles inland to this dry room with its shut windows, and she had the manifest in her coat, and she was not going to be sorted into a category by a man with a ruler.

Chapter four won't start

Maya Ferreira opened this 3 days ago · 400–900 words · 2 suggestions

I know where they end up and I can't get them off the boat. Every opening I write is the ship landing, and the ship isn't supposed to land — that's the whole point of the next three chapters. I need the first eight hundred words of a chapter where they decide not to.

The story so far, in Maya's words

Ellery Sabin crosses three hundred miles of dry country to register a ship that has no name, because naming it would admit it exists. She's carrying a manifest she stole from her brother, who is on the ship, who does not know she's coming. The crew have been at sea for nine months on a two-month crossing and have stopped counting. By the end of chapter three the ship is close enough to shore that the crew can smell woodsmoke, and the captain has just told them she has no intention of putting in.

Things that can't change

  • Stay in third person, close on Ellery — she's not on the ship, so we're watching from shore
  • Nobody dies in chapter four
  • The captain's reason stays unspoken until chapter six

The woodsmoke reached the shore before the ship did, and then it kept reaching, and the ship did not.

Ellery had been standing on the sea wall for two hours by then, long enough that the fishermen had stopped asking, and she had worked out the arithmetic of it without wanting to. A ship that intends to land gets larger. This one had been the same size since noon.

She thought about the manifest in her coat and how little it would mean to anyone who never came ashore to read it…
